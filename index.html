The problem

Publishing is a taste business with a formatting problem.

Most of what a publisher does is mechanical: extract, typeset, generate metadata, build keyword lists, produce an EPUB, localise a glossary. Software has been able to do all of it for years.

The part that isn't mechanical is judgement. Which cover makes someone stop scrolling. Whether a blurb lands. Whether a translated sentence sounds like a person wrote it. That's why publishers charge what they charge — you're paying for taste, bundled with a lot of formatting.

Bookit unbundles it. Agents do the formatting for near-zero cost. Taste gets bought by the question, from real readers, through an API.

Traditional publisher

Self-publishing alone

How it works

Four steps, and one honest admission.

1

You upload, and you talk

Drop in a PDF and tell Bookit about your book in plain language — genre, who it's for, what you want, what you can spend, what language you want it in. The more specific you are, the more specific the plan.

2

An agent writes your publishing plan

Not a checklist. A plan built for your book, as a set of action items — each one priced, timed, and labelled with who does it: Agent or Human via Terac. You pick what you want. Nothing runs without your approval.

3

Agents do the mechanical work

Text extraction, a named-entity glossary so translations stay consistent, print-ready typesetting, a reflowable EPUB, store metadata and keywords, cover directions, launch copy. This is the part that used to cost you months.

4

Then it hires humans for the taste calls

Bookit posts the judgement calls to Terac, a network of 180,000+ people. Real readers rank your covers, your blurbs, your titles. Native speakers check your translation. Bookit ships what they chose — not what the model preferred.

The honest admission: a model can generate a hundred covers, but it cannot tell you which one a human would pick up in a bookstore. Anyone who says otherwise is selling you a guess. Bookit's whole design is built around that limit rather than hiding it — which is why the interesting number on this page is in Human results, not in the demo.

        Why this changes the product

        Bookit's cover ranking is a model's aesthetic preference. A reader's choice is a purchase signal. When those two disagree — and they did — the reader wins and the book ships differently. That gap is the product: it's the difference between a book that looks generated and a book that looks published.

        How it's built

        The stack.

        LayerWhat it does
        Terac MCPRecruits and pays the human panel. Bookit's agent creates a project, launches an opportunity with screening questions targeted at general readers, then polls for results and acts on them. This is the only part of the system that has taste.
        Planning agentReads the manuscript and the author's own words, extracts intent (language, platform, genre, audience, budget), and emits a priced, sequenced action plan with each item routed to either an agent or a human.
        Production agentsText extraction, named-entity glossary, typesetting, EPUB generation, cover synthesis, metadata and keyword research, launch copy.
        StripeThe agent invoices the author directly with a payment link. No human on Bookit's side sends an invoice.
        ReplayAutomated QA. Explores the app, finds bugs, reports root causes — our test suite, written by nobody.

        Why this is a "zero human company": nobody at Bookit reads your manuscript, designs your cover, writes your blurb, or sends your invoice. Humans are still essential — but they enter as a priced, on-demand input the agent buys when it hits the edge of its own judgement, not as staff. That's the bet: the company has no employees, and still has 180,000 people working for it.
